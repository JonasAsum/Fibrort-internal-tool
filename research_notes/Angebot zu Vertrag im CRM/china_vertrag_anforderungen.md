# Sales contracts and order confirmations for mainland China B2B customers, generated from a Hong Kong self-hosted CRM (state 2026; integrator information, not legal advice)

Method note: sources are mostly law-firm and vendor pages reached via search summaries; several primary pages returned 403 or unreadable PDFs (Latham PDF, chinalawtranslate, Harris Sliwoski chop article). Statements marked "own knowledge" are not source-backed and must be verified with PRC counsel.

## 1. Which fields must a sales contract contain at minimum, and which can come from a CRM template?

### Takeaway
The Civil Code lists typical clauses (Art. 470) as a guide, not a validity checklist; a document is a contract once it shows mutual consent with parties, subject matter and quantity. A CRM can fill nearly all of it automatically except authority-to-sign evidence and negotiated clauses (liability, dispute resolution).

### Cited Findings
- Art. 470 Civil Code typical contents: parties' names and domiciles, subject matter, quantity, quality, price, performance terms, liability for breach, dispute resolution; a guide, not a checklist that voids a contract if an item is missing — [CMS summary of SPC interpretation](https://cms.law/en/media/local/cms-china/files/publications/newsletters/supreme-people-s-court-interpretation-on-general-provisions-of-the-contract-part-of-the-prc-civil-code-released) (via search summary; page not fetched)
- A document is generally a contract if it reflects mutual consent and clearly states parties' names, subject matter and quantity — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/navigating-contract-management-in-china-key-insights-from-the-judicial-interpretation-on-contract-in-the-civil-code/)
- Purchase orders may lack signature/chop; consent can follow from emails, acknowledgments or performance (e.g. seller starts production); recommended: name both parties, state subject matter, quantity, price, delivery, keep records of trade practice — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/navigating-contract-management-in-china-key-insights-from-the-judicial-interpretation-on-contract-in-the-civil-code/)
- Pre-drafted "standard terms" (repeated use, e.g. a CRM template) that limit liability or the counterparty's rights can be invalidated if the provider did not reasonably highlight and, on request, explain them; use prominent keywords/symbols/fonts; for online contracts tick-boxes/pop-ups are generally not enough; keep evidence of explanations — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/navigating-contract-management-in-china-key-insights-from-the-judicial-interpretation-on-contract-in-the-civil-code/)
- Signer authority: legal representative's signature generally binds the company; restrictions in articles don't bind a bona fide counterparty; staff authority tied to function and may be "beyond scope" — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/navigating-contract-management-in-china-key-insights-from-the-judicial-interpretation-on-contract-in-the-civil-code/)
- Bilingual: with no governing-language clause a Chinese court/arbitration panel tends to treat the Chinese text as prevailing; Chinese version relied on especially if filed with local authorities; prevailing-language clauses may not prevent disputes where texts contradict; counsel often recommend Chinese as prevailing if meticulously translated — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/china/contracts-and-commercial-law/1182392/china-business-contract-dos-and-donts), [Wr.no](https://www.wr.no/en/news/how-to-handle-bilingual-contracts)

### Inferences
- Field set for the template (CRM-fillable): seller legal name ZH+EN, address, contact; buyer full registered Chinese name, USCC, registered address, legal representative, contact; contract no., date, place of signing; line items (name, model/spec, unit, qty, unit price, amount); VAT rate and tax-inclusive/exclusive statement; total in figures and Chinese capital words (大写, own knowledge, common practice); currency (RMB vs USD/HKD, own knowledge); delivery terms/Incoterm, place, date; payment terms/bank details; quality/acceptance and inspection period; warranty; liability for breach / liquidated damages; force majeure; governing law; dispute resolution (court or arbitration, e.g. CIETAC/HKIAC, own knowledge); language clause; signature/chop block; number of originals.
- Fields needing human input per deal: liability caps, penalty rates, dispute forum, acceptance period, signer authority proof.
- Highlight (bold/colour) liability-limiting clauses in the generated document and log delivery of the explanation, because of the standard-terms rule.
- Buyer's Chinese name must match the business licence exactly (chop carries the Chinese name; own knowledge) — store ZH name as a separate mandatory CRM field.

### Gaps
- Official Civil Code article text (470, 496, 584) not fetched; Art. 584 content only from memory (damages limited to foreseeable loss) — verify.
- Whether a specific mandatory clause list applies to cross-border-sales-from-HK contracts (e.g. CISG applicability; HK seller vs mainland buyer) not researched; note CISG applies to China and, via China, to HK/PRC parties (own knowledge, unverified).
- Tax-inclusive price rules and seller-side requirements for contracts with a HK entity as seller (goods import VAT/customs, who is importer of record) not researched.

## 2. What is legally accepted for signing; what do Chinese B2B customers expect?

### Takeaway
Wet signature plus company chop remains the safest and still common; a "reliable" electronic signature/e-chop has equal legal effect, and parties can agree on the signature method. Chinese counterparties commonly use local platforms (Fadada, e-Qianbao); DocuSign has weak local fit.

### Cited Findings
- Electronic contracts agreed by both parties cannot be denied validity solely for being electronic; a reliable e-signature has the same effect as handwritten signature or seal — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/electronic-contracts-china-can-improve-efficiency-strong-controls/), [Zegal](https://zegal.com/en-hk/blog/post/e-sign-laws-china/)
- Reliability: signing data belongs exclusively to signatory; signatory alone controls it at signing; later alterations to signature and to content are detectable (Electronic Signature Law Art. 13) — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/electronic-contracts-china-can-improve-efficiency-strong-controls/)
- Parties may agree on their own reliable e-signature conditions — [Zegal](https://zegal.com/en-hk/blog/post/e-sign-laws-china/) (via search summary)
- Exclusions: personal-relationship documents, immovable property transfers, public-service termination, other laws-excluded items — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/electronic-contracts-china-can-improve-efficiency-strong-controls/)
- Third-party CA and timestamp authority not legally required but recommended; keep evidence of creation and communications — [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/electronic-contracts-china-can-improve-efficiency-strong-controls/)
- E-chop treated as equivalent to physical chop if signing-data control/detectability criteria met; courts have held companies liable for e-chop misuse; counterparties should verify legitimacy of creation data; Art. 28 liability of verification service — [Harris Sliwoski via search summary](https://harris-sliwoski.com/chinalawblog/the-chinese-company-chop-goes-digital/) (page itself returned 403)
- Contract signed by legal representative or authorised staff generally effective without chop unless contract requires chop; any agreement affixed with company chop is generally binding regardless of who used it; best practice both signature and chop, and check legal representative against business licence — [Harris Sliwoski via search summary](https://harris-sliwoski.com/chinalawblog/china-contracts-that-work-get-the-company-chop-right/), [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/navigating-contract-management-in-china-key-insights-from-the-judicial-interpretation-on-contract-in-the-civil-code/)
- Fadada's global product is Nota Sign; Tradelink became exclusive HK distributor (Oct 2025); vendor claims HK data centre, SES/AES/QES support, ISO 27001/27701 (vendor self-description) — [Tradelink](https://www.tradelink.com.hk/en/solutions/identity-management/fadada-digital-signing/), [Tradelink news](https://www.tradelink.com.hk/en/success-stories/identity-management-fadada-nota-sign/)
- Competitor blog (eSign.ai, biased) claims DocuSign is not banned in China but poorly suited without local deployment, and Adobe Sign exited China — [eSign.ai](https://www.esign.ai/es-MX/blog/is-docusign-allowed-in-mainland-china) (low-reliability source)

### Inferences
- Pragmatic flow for an integrator: generate PDF from CRM, export for signing by (a) wet signature + physical chop scanned/couriered, or (b) integration with a local platform (Fadada/Nota Sign, e-Qianbao/eSignGlobal-type) producing a reliable e-signature; keep audit trail + hash of the PDF in the CRM.
- Order confirmations (not a full contract) can plausibly rely on the PO/acknowledgment-by-conduct logic, but combine with a framework agreement incl. signed T&Cs for the standard-terms risk.
- Many Chinese B2B buyers prefer chop on paper or their own platform's e-chop (own knowledge, no cited source): offering both options is safest.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for e-Qianbao (e签宝) or Fadada API/pricing/legal-certification details; no independent source on DocuSign availability for mainland signers; no data on what share of Chinese B2B buyers accept e-signatures from foreign (HK) sellers.
- Whether a foreign (HK) party can sign with a Chinese platform without a mainland business entity/real-name verification not established.
- Also unresolved: recognition of HK-CA (Digi-Sign) certificates in mainland courts.

## 3. Hosting in Hong Kong: PIPL, cross-border transfer, exemptions

### Takeaway
Mainland-collected personal data stored in HK counts as an outbound transfer under mainland law if the collector is in the mainland; a HK-based seller collecting directly from mainland B2B contacts is governed by PDPO in HK, but PIPL Art. 3 may reach it extraterritorially (B2B scope unsettled). The 2024 CAC Provisions make most small-volume cases exempt from filing.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 CAC Provisions effective 22 March 2024: no transfer mechanism needed for non-sensitive PI of fewer than 100,000 individuals exported since 1 January of the year; 100,000-1 million non-sensitive or up to 10,000 sensitive: standard contract filing or certification; above: security assessment; exemptions for contract necessity (contract with the individual as party), HR management, emergencies; important data not exempted — [Latham/Gide/Fangda summaries via search](https://www.gide.com/sites/default/files/gide_china_client_alert_china_eases_cross-border_data_transfer_controls_apr2024.pdf) (not opened), [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/china-releases-new-rules-to-ease-burden-on-cross-border-transfer-of-data); boundary wording ("more than" vs "not less than") differs between translations
- Contract-necessity exemption covers contracts with individuals as a party; B2B contact persons are not the contracting party, so applicability is doubtful — summary per search; (reasoning in Inferences)
- Before the 2024 relaxation the routes were security assessment, certification, or CAC-filed standard contract; PIPL effective 1 Nov 2021 — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/china-streamlines-requirements-regarding-data-export-in-the-greater-bay-area)
- Hong Kong: PDPO (Cap. 486) with six DPPs; no government filing for outbound transfers; section 33 transfer restriction still not in force, no timetable — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/alerts/china-streamlines-requirements-regarding-data-export-in-the-greater-bay-area), [PCPD](https://www.pcpd.org.hk/english/whatsnew/20240314.html) (via search)
- GBA Standard Contract (13 Dec 2023, CAC + HK ITIB + PCPD): voluntary, covers transfers between HK and nine GBA mainland cities (Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Foshan, Huizhou, Dongguan, Zhongshan, Jiangmen, Zhaoqing) — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2023/china/part-1-all-you-need-to-know-about-the-standard-contract-for-gba-data-transfers)
- PIPL does not directly apply in HK but has extraterritorial effect (Art. 3: offering products/services to individuals in China, or analysing their behaviour); B2B coverage unclear, no Chinese authority clarification as of the sources (mostly 2021); if covered, a representative/entity in PRC is required; fines up to RMB 50 million or 5% of turnover — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/211025-china-personal-information-protection-law-faqs), [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/PIPL-FAQs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Data categories in the CRM: buyer contact names, phones, emails of employees = personal information; USCC, company name, address = company data, not PI (own knowledge). Volume for a small CRM is far below 100,000 individuals, so the filing threshold is not reached; consent/notice and minimisation duties remain.
- Documents holding legal-representative names/IDs or signatory ID scans (from e-signature real-name verification) may be sensitive/ID data: avoid storing ID scans in the HK CRM.
- Practical setup: keep an internal notice to customer contacts (privacy statement, HK processing), limit PI fields, document the HK hosting in a record of processing; ask counsel whether the Chinese subsidiary (if it is the collector) must treat HK transfers as exports.

### Gaps
- Official CAC Art. 5 text not retrieved; whether B2B business-contact data collected by a mainland entity and sent to its HK parent needs a mechanism not answered by sources.
- Any 2025-2026 developments (new CAC rules, PDPO amendments, GBA updates) not checked beyond 2024-2025 firm alerts.
- Whether the importer of record / the contracting seller is a mainland WFOE or a HK company is unknown and decides which regime applies.

## 4. Tax: VAT rates and fapiao fields

### Takeaway
VAT Law in force 1 Jan 2026: rates 13/9/6/0; the contract should state the rate and whether prices include tax; the buyer's USCC is needed for fapiao; e-fapiao nationwide.

### Cited Findings
- VAT Law (adopted 25 Dec 2024, in force 1 Jan 2026), Art. 10 rates: 13% goods/processing/repair/movable-property leasing/imports; 9% transport, postal, basic telecom, construction, immovable leasing/sales and certain staples; 6% other services and intangibles; 0% exports and qualifying cross-border services; Implementing Regulations Decree No. 826 same date — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/china)
- Small-scale taxpayers (up to RMB 5 million annual sales): levy rate 3%, reduced to 1% 1 Jan 2026-31 Dec 2027 for most transactions per MOF/STA Announcement No. 10 of 2026 — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/china)
- Special VAT fapiao enables input-tax credit and cannot be issued to individuals; ordinary fapiao for enterprise buyers must show buyer taxpayer ID/USCC (STA Announcement No. 16 of 2017) — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/china)
- Mandatory fapiao fields (STA Announcement No. 11 of 2024): 20-digit number, date, buyer name and ID/USCC, seller name and ID/USCC, item name, spec, unit, qty, unit price, amount, tax rate, tax amount, totals, remarks, issuer — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/china)
- Fully digitalised e-fapiao rolled out nationwide from 1 Dec 2024 with same legal effect as paper; paper still valid — [LookupTax](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/china); formats OFD/PDF/XML for B2B per [OhMyFin](https://ohmyfin.ai/tr/invoice/requirements/china) (low-quality source)

### Inferences
- CRM line items should carry name, spec/model, unit, quantity, unit price, tax rate: the same data set later feeds fapiao issuance, so use it in the contract.
- Add checkbox "price includes VAT at x%; seller issues special VAT fapiao" plus buyer fapiao data block (company name, USCC, address, phone, bank, account).
- If the seller is a HK company, it cannot issue Chinese fapiao itself; the invoicing and tax flow (exports/imports, 0% or customs VAT) depends on the structure — own knowledge, unverified.

### Gaps
- Not researched: fapiao for HK-seller scenarios, customs/import VAT, withholding taxes, stamp duty on sales contracts (own knowledge: stamp duty exists under the Stamp Tax Law, rate for sale contracts 0.03% - unverified).

## 5. Delivery, Incoterms, payment terms, and company registration data

### Takeaway
No dedicated sources found; recommend template fields only.

### Cited Findings
- None source-backed beyond Art. 470 item list (performance terms) cited above.

### Inferences
- Use Incoterms 2020 with named place (e.g. FCA, CIF, DAP; "DDP" mainland is rare for HK sellers, own knowledge); state who handles customs clearance, risk transfer, delivery date, partial shipments, acceptance and inspection period, price validity.
- Payment: currency, deposit percentage, balance trigger (against delivery or acceptance), bank details (RMB account vs offshore), late-payment interest, title retention (所有权保留) clause. Cross-border RMB or USD payments need compliant routing (own knowledge).
- USCC is an 18-character code; implement a format and check-digit validation (GB 32100-2015, own knowledge) and capture the Chinese registered name from the licence; the NECIPS/Qichacha lookup could verify (not researched).

### Gaps
- No sources on Incoterms use in China contracts, payment-term norms, or USCC validation; all items above are own knowledge.

## 6. Open-source / low-cost rendering of bilingual PDF or docx from templates

### Takeaway
docxtpl (Jinja2 inside a .docx) plus LibreOffice headless PDF conversion is the pragmatic route; the main risk is CJK font availability in the server or container. HTML-to-PDF (WeasyPrint, wkhtmltopdf) is the alternative.

### Cited Findings
- docxtpl: Jinja2 tags inside Word, paragraph/row/cell/run-level tags, loops and conditions, tables with merge, RichText, InlineImage (images in body; in headers/footers only replaceable), subdocuments; Jinja tags must stay within one run; `<`, `>`, `&` need escaping; licence not stated on the fetched page — [docxtpl docs](https://docxtpl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
- LibreOffice headless: CJK glyph "tofu" boxes were reported in PDF export even with Noto CJK installed (LibreOffice 7 regression reported; downgrade to 6.4.7.2 fixed it for that reporter); headless fontconfig substitution was added in a later commit; a slim Docker image deliberately omits fonts-noto-cjk, so CJK needs explicit installation — [LibreOffice bug 142667](https://bugs.libreoffice.org/show_bug.cgi?id=142667), [freedesktop bug 49035](https://bugs.freedesktop.org/show_bug.cgi?id=49035), [Docker image](https://hub.docker.com/r/ofthemachine/libreoffice) (via search summaries)
- WeasyPrint: GitHub issue reports `local()` in @font-face failing for a CJK font (mojibake/warning); workaround of loading the font file via URL is an inference from the report — [Lightrun mirror](https://lightrun.com/answers/kozea-weasyprint-cant-load-font-using-local)
- Noto Sans CJK registers as family "Noto Sans CJK SC" in fontconfig on distros; use `fc-list` to confirm names; Asciidoctor PDF notes TTF fonts are needed for its toolchain and Noto Sans SC has a thin weight — [Asciidoctor docs](https://docs.asciidoctor.org/pdf-converter/2.1/theme/cjk/)

### Inferences
- Recommended pipeline: docx template (EN/ZH side-by-side table, two columns per clause, or paired paragraphs) -> docxtpl fill -> `soffice --headless --convert-to pdf` -> PDF/A for archiving if needed; install fonts-noto-cjk (or Source Han Sans / Noto Serif CJK for formal Songti look) and run `fc-cache -f`; set the font explicitly in the template (e.g. Noto Sans CJK SC / SimSun fallback mapping); embed fonts; visually test a CJK sample in CI.
- Layout: two-column bilingual table is easiest to keep paired and review; amounts in figures plus Chinese capital numerals; Chinese text first if Chinese is to prevail.
- Version-pin LibreOffice and test after upgrades, given reported CJK regressions.
- Where the contract goes to an e-sign platform, supply a flattened PDF and keep the docx as source; place signature/chop fields via anchor text.
- Whether wkhtmltopdf is advisable: project reported as archived/unmaintained (own knowledge, not verified in this research); prefer WeasyPrint or headless Chromium.

### Gaps
- No tested comparison of WeasyPrint vs Chromium vs LibreOffice for CJK in this research; docxtpl licence (LGPL, own knowledge) not verified; no cost data for hosted conversion services.
